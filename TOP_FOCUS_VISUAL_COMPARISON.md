# Top Focus Bar Design - Visual Comparison

## New Layout Philosophy

**Top focus bar** = Prime visual real estate for the "Big Three"
**Main section** = Schedule + Tasks + Notes

All options use:
- ✅ Hours: 6-20 (15 hours)
- ✅ 6 task checkboxes
- ✅ ~23 note lines
- ✅ Full-width top bar with 3 columns (or 4 in Option 4)
- ✅ 3 lines per column for each focus item

## Usage Scenarios

### Choose **Option 1** if:
- You like explicit, clear labeling
- You're teaching/sharing the planner format
- You prefer maximum clarity over minimalism
- You want no ambiguity about what goes where

### Choose **Option 2** ⭐ if:
- You want the best balance of clarity and minimalism
- You like the "Big Three" mindset
- Simple numbers (1, 2, 3) are enough guidance
- You want clean aesthetics without sacrificing usability
- **This is the sweet spot for most users**

### Choose **Option 3** if:
- You're a minimalist who wants zero labels
- You know exactly how you'll use the space
- You prefer maximum flexibility
- The concept of "Focus" vs "Additional Tasks" appeals to you
- You don't need visual cues

### Choose **Option 4** if:
- You consistently have 4 top priorities per day
- You're okay with narrower columns (might be cramped on A5)
- You want more granular top-level tracking
- You don't mind slightly tighter spacing

### 🏆 **Option 2: "Today's Big Three" with Numbers**

This is the goldilocks option:

**Not too prescriptive** (like Option 1's "Priority 1/2/3")
**Not too sparse** (like Option 3's no labels)
**Not too cramped** (like Option 4's 4 columns)

**Just right:**
- ✅ "Big Three" reinforces the productivity concept
- ✅ Numbers (1, 2, 3) provide ordering without being verbose
- ✅ Clean, modern aesthetic
- ✅ Perfect for A5 paper (~4.7cm per column)
- ✅ Easy to scan and use daily
- ✅ Implicit priority without being prescriptive
